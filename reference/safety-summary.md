# Safety summary — liability in the example report

What Switch would be signing if the excavator report follows Comptune Engineering report B2600003A. Drawn from the signed text of that report (Bradley Aiken, 3 July 2026) and from [`docs/sow.md`](../docs/sow.md).

Switch takes this on only if a Switch engineer signs a report in that form. Nothing in the October 2026 correspondence has done that yet. The draft statement of work commits Switch to a report in the form of B2600003A.

## The opinion

The summary in B2600003A says the install method “could be used on machines with a similar braking system that is hydraulically actuated,” provided the kits are fitted with care and function is verified by a test drive after installation.

That is a suitability opinion for a class of machines, not a note limited to Komatsu WA470-8 serial 100804. HSE Pacific’s stated use of the report is to satisfy Rio Tinto before a wider rollout of the same method. A later machine that does not stop as expected is the claim this sentence supports.

## Claims about the brakes

The findings state that:

- The original brake system “remains unaltered” and the OEM service-brake set pressures stay as they were, because the solenoid is teed in after the accumulator.
- A camera detection applies the service brakes at a reduced flow (the example used is about 50%), and the operator can still press the pedal and get a greater flow.
- After fitment, a test drive both proves the system and sets the solenoid knob for the machine’s weight and speed.

Those are safety-critical claims: the modification does not change the OEM brakes, and it cannot override the operator. They are also the claims least likely to survive a move from that loader’s hydraulic brake circuit to a Liebherr 922, where the stopping system may be travel brake, slew brake, or a request on the machine’s existing controls rather than a tee into a service-brake line.

## The standard

The report names AS 3450:2021 as “the relevant standard” and says it “has been reviewed against this modification.” It does not say the modification complies, and it records no brake performance test from that standard. Naming the standard in the cover block, next to a signed suitability conclusion, is what a reader will treat as a compliance review.

AS 3450:2021 applies to wheeled or high-speed rubber-tracked machines. Putting it on the excavator report in the same way would be reviewing a standard that does not cover the machine.

## What the signature covers

The report does not assess whether the cameras detect a person, how often they miss, or what happens if the added circuit fails. It does not approve a fleet, a design, or a regulatory certification.

The signature block is the engineer’s own name plus a Vehicle Safety Compliance Certification Scheme number and an Engineers Australia number. The draft statement of work asks for the same kind of block: the inspecting Switch engineer’s signature and professional registration. That is personal professional responsibility for the words above, used by HSE Pacific as the document Rio Tinto sees before rollout.

## Liability assumed

The liability sits in two places. Switch Technologies (WA) Pty Ltd carries it as the contracting party. The engineer who signs carries it as the author of the opinion. The statement of work is not yet executed. Its Master Services Agreement date is still a placeholder, so no agreed cap applies to this job yet.

### Switch Technologies (WA) Pty Ltd

Switch is the party to the statement of work. Its liability to HSE Pacific is for performing the inspection and for the report as the deliverable. The engineer does that work in the course of the engagement, and Switch answers to the client for it.

A cap, an exclusion, and an insurance obligation would sit in the Master Services Agreement. None of those is agreed in the October correspondence. A cap agreed later with HSE Pacific would limit claims by HSE Pacific. Rio Tinto is not a party to that contract, and nor is a person injured by the machine. An exemption agreed with the client does not bind either of them.

The out-of-scope list keeps design, supply, installation, a fleet rollout, and a regulatory type approval off the contracted work. The liability that remains is the opinion. The sentence that the method is suitable for machines with a similar arrangement is the part of the deliverable a rollout will use.

Rio Tinto has asked for a certificate of currency for liability, and for indemnity where it applies. The statement of work agrees to provide the certificates the site requires. Those certificates evidence cover. They do not set the amount of the opinion. The limit of indemnity on Switch’s policy is not recorded in this repository. Confirming that this opinion sits inside that policy, and for what limit, is a step before anyone signs.

### The signing engineer

The engineer is not a party to the statement of work. The report is signed in the engineer’s own name, with the registration they hold, and the date. That signature is the author adopting the findings and the conclusion.

An engineer can owe a duty of care to people who are not the client, where those people are foreseeably harmed by the work. In *Voli v Inglewood Shire Council* (1963) 110 CLR 74, an architect was liable to a person injured when a stage he had certified collapsed. The duty sat alongside his contract with the council, and the contract did not protect him from the injured person. The same shape applies here. HSE Pacific is the client. The report’s known purpose is to satisfy Rio Tinto before the system is fitted more widely, and the system exists to stop a machine short of a person.

Employment does not take the name off the signature. Switch remains responsible to the client for the engagement. The engineer remains the person who stated that the brakes were unaltered, that the operator could still stop harder than the kit, and that the method could be used on similar machines. A claim can name both. The firm’s professional indemnity policy is the cover that ordinarily responds for an employee who signs in the course of the engagement. That response depends on the policy wording and the limit, which are not in this repository.

In Western Australia the standard expected of that professional work is competent practice widely accepted by peer opinion in Australia, unless that opinion is irrational (*Civil Liability Act 2002* (WA) s 5PB). That standard judges the care taken in the work. A finding of fact still has to be one the inspection supports. “The original brake system remains unaltered” is a finding of fact. “The method could be used on similar machines” is the opinion.

Western Australia does not generally licence professional engineers. An Engineers Australia number is membership. The number on the example report also includes a Vehicle Safety Compliance Certification Scheme appointment, which is a personal function for certifying modified vehicles in New South Wales. The statement of work asks the Switch engineer to sign with the professional registration they hold.

For economic loss and property damage caused by a failure to take reasonable care, Western Australian proportionate liability can divide responsibility between Switch, HSE Pacific, and anyone else whose want of care contributed (*Civil Liability Act 2002* (WA) Part 1F). A personal injury claim sits outside that division. The injured person can recover the whole loss from one defendant, who then seeks contribution from the others.

The *Work Health and Safety Act 2020* (WA) puts the primary duty on Switch as the person conducting the business. A designer of plant has a separate duty to ensure, so far as is reasonably practicable, that the plant is without risks to health and safety. Design of the kit is outside this statement of work. A conclusion that an install method is suitable for a class of machines is the point at which the report can be read as design advice on the modification.

### What the signature takes responsibility for

Signing a report in the form of B2600003A assumes responsibility, to the standard of a careful inspecting engineer, for these propositions:

- The sample machine was inspected, and the findings describe what was fitted and how it joins the stopping system.
- The original brakes and their set pressures are unchanged by the installation.
- The operator can still command a stop beyond the installation.
- The named standard was reviewed against the modification.
- The method is suitable for machines with a similar arrangement, on the conditions of fitment and a verification drive.

It assumes those sentences are fit to be relied on by HSE Pacific, and by Rio Tinto for the purpose Switch has been told the report will serve.

Design of the kit, detection performance of the cameras, the quality of later installations, and Rio Tinto’s decision to roll the system out stay off the signature while the report says so in terms. The example report leaves detection performance unstated, and its suitability sentence is how one loader becomes a method for other machines.

## Limits already in the draft statement of work

The draft statement of work limits the opinion to the sample machine and to the same method on machines with a similar arrangement, subject to fitment and verification. It requires the report to name the standard reviewed for the excavator and to describe the stopping arrangement actually found.

Those limits hold only if they are written into the signed report. A report that copies the loader’s “unaltered brakes,” “operator can still brake harder,” and “AS 3450 reviewed” sentences would assume those claims for the 922.
